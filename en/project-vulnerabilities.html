P-02Personal project

Vulnerability prioritisation

A high severity score doesn't mean a flaw is really dangerous. This tool combines three signals to separate the real from the theoretical, using real data from the CISA KEV catalogue.

  • 3combined signals: CVSS, EPSS and business criticality
  • 30vulnerabilities ranked on the demo set
  • P1 to P4priority levels, each with a score out of 100
Demo data set
01

The problem

A high severity score doesn't mean a flaw is really dangerous. Sorting fixes on that number alone means spending time on theoretical flaws while another one is being actively exploited.

02

The thinking: why three signals

SignalWhat it tells youSource
Technical severityWhat a flaw would allow in theoryCVSS (NVD)
Likelihood of exploitationWhether anyone is actually exploiting itEPSS (FIRST.org)
Business criticalityWhether the affected system matters to the companyCompany context

None of the three is enough on its own: combining them gives the order in which to fix. The demo data comes from the CISA KEV catalogue of vulnerabilities known to be exploited.

What the tool produces

A dashboard with the breakdown by priority, a table of every flaw with its score out of 100, and a remediation plan in Excel and PDF. On the demo set: 30 vulnerabilities ranked from P1 to P4.
