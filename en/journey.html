A journey, not a CV.

From Benin to Côte d'Ivoire, by way of La Rochelle and Paris: how I came to cybersecurity.

§ 01 · Origins

Understanding how things work.

As a child in Benin, an object that stopped working was never the end of the story. I would take it apart, look inside and try to understand the logic of the mechanism before putting it back in working order. A silent radio, a toy that wouldn't switch on: without ever deciding to, I became the person the whole household turned to whenever something broke down.

That curiosity found its place in computing, and above all in a contest that has always fascinated me: on one side, those who try to get into a system; on the other, those who learn to stop them. It wasn't one career choice among others. It was the continuation of what I already knew how to do.

I did my two years of preparatory classes in Benin. I finished the last one on my own: my father had been posted to Côte d'Ivoire and my whole family had joined him. Interrupting my studies was not an option, so I stayed in Cotonou to finish what I had started.

I don't tell this story to impress. That year is when independence, which had been a personality trait, became a skill I truly practised. I still rely on it, whether running a project almost independently at MTN or building an application end to end on my own.
